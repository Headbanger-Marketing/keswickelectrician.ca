Before work begins

Find out what was done before you build on it.

Did you buy your home recently? The previous owner's changes may be unknown, circuit labels may not match what they control, and the purchase inspection report may mention items nobody can explain or vouch for. Any home can hide unrecorded work.

Gather the inspection report, any permits and ESA paperwork, and invoices for earlier work. Where no record exists, note that plainly in your enquiry, so the contractor can plan an assessment before relying on or building around the existing wiring.

Where do I look for records of earlier electrical work?

Start with the seller's disclosure, the inspection report, invoices and any ESA paperwork you were given. Ask the previous owner or the seller's agent for contacts, and remember that a missing record does not show whether work was done properly.

Must a panel with no known history be swapped out?

Not necessarily. Unknown history is a reason for assessment, not proof that equipment is faulty. Ask the contractor what was observed, what could not be seen and whether replacement, repair or no change is the finding, with reasons in writing.

What should two quotes say about wiring nobody has documented?

Check that each quote states the same assumptions about concealed wiring and says what happens if something unexpected is found. A quote that ignores unknown conditions may change later, so ask each contractor how they would handle that.

Who can authorize electrical work and sign for it after a purchase?

The owner you name on the enquiry usually authorizes the work, but a condominium corporation or tenant arrangement may add approvals. Have your purchase documents ready, and tell the contractor who can approve changes and who can open the door.

If I send the form about a hot outlet, who arranges the visit?

Any contractor you deal with, not this planning site. It reviews your enquiry, has no say in pricing, visits or the work itself, and neither dispatches anyone nor responds to urgent faults. A Licensed Electrical Contractor must give its name and licence separately, then work out the job and its price directly with you, and when work happens.
