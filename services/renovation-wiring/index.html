Renovation wiring in Keswick.

Renovation wiring in a Keswick home has to fit the schedule of a general contractor and other trades. When earlier electrical work is unrecorded, as it may be in a home you recently bought, the opened walls may bring surprises.

Electrical decisions belong early in the renovation

A renovation changes how the space will be used and what equipment it must serve. Provide the drawings and appliance specifications before the electrical scope is finalized. A Licensed Electrical Contractor can assess retained wiring, new circuits and the proposed loads. Municipal building permissions and ESA electrical notification are separate matters, and the project may require both.

Prepare the scope for your Keswick property

List every trade involved, the order they will work in and who talks to whom. Note where walls or ceilings will be opened, and photograph any existing electrical boxes or cables that you can see without disturbing them.

Ask who is responsible for each step, how unexpected wiring found behind an opened wall will be reported to you, and who arranges the ESA notification and any inspection that follows.

What should the assessment establish?

An estimate that lists new circuits for a Keswick renovation and says nothing of what is already behind the walls leaves open which parts stay and when other trades may close up. Existing wiring behind the work area is described, with the parts the project keeps. The scope sequences electrical work with framing, insulation and finishing so nothing is covered before review. If undocumented wiring turns up, the contractor explains what happens, who decides and who pays for the change in scope. The scope separates electrical notification from any building permit the municipality requires. The person authorized to approve changes is named.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Who decides what to do if hidden wiring turns up mid-renovation?

Agree this before work starts. Ask the electrical contractor and the general contractor to name one person who decides, how the finding will be documented with photographs, and how the scope and schedule would change.

Does wiring have to be finished before insulation and drywall go up?

Concealed wiring generally needs to be in place and reviewed before it is covered, but the sequence is set in the project plan. Ask the contractors to agree it in writing and to say when a review is expected.

Prepare your enquiry

Useful project details

  • Drawings and intended use of each space
  • Appliance and equipment model information
  • Construction schedule and other trades
  • Existing installation records if available
  • Person authorized to approve changes

Only photograph labels and locations that you can see safely. Do not remove electrical covers or approach damaged equipment to gather information.
