Electrical safety assessment in Keswick.

An insurer question about wiring or a panel in a Keswick home needs a clear, written answer. Earlier work with no records makes it more important to agree what the assessment will cover.

A useful assessment has a stated purpose and boundary

A condition concern, renovation decision or insurer request can each call for a different assessment. Agree which equipment and areas will be reviewed, whether testing is included and what written result will be provided. Concealed wiring and inaccessible equipment may limit conclusions. A general visit should not be represented as complete certification of everything in the property.

Prepare the scope for your Keswick property

Find the insurer's letter or form and note exactly what it asks, by when and in what format. Gather the inspection report and any earlier paperwork you hold, and mark the items that mention wiring or the panel.

Ask the contractor whether the written result can address the insurer's wording directly, what parts of the installation will be reviewed and which concealed parts cannot be reached to support any statement.

What should the assessment establish?

What has the Keswick owner's insurer asked about the wiring or panel? The report takes that question as its stated purpose, in plain language rather than a general opinion, and lists the areas reviewed and the limits of what could be seen. How were the wiring and panel found? They are described as they stood, with no assumption that unknown earlier work is faulty or sound. Was testing included? The contractor says so either way and marks the findings that need further work. The report also notes that acceptance stays with the insurer and that ESA review is a separate matter.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Will an electrical safety assessment satisfy what my insurer asked?

That is for the insurer to decide. An assessment can give you a written account of what was reviewed and found, so ask the insurer what form it wants before you ask the contractor to prepare it.

What if the contractor finds work that no one can explain?

Ask for it to be described and photographed, with a statement of what could be confirmed and what could not. The contractor can advise on next steps, and any correction should be scoped separately before it proceeds.
